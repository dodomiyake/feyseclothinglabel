# Feyse Clothing Labels — "Signature" · Treatment

**Style:** Blueprint (white line work on Prussian blue, one sheet, drafting order).
**Length / format:** 48 s · 1920×1080 · 24 fps · warm female narrator (English) · burned-in note-style subtitles + `.srt`.

## 1. Three candidate structures

1. **Drafting order → order journey (chosen).** One woven label is drawn the way an engineer would draw it: centre line, outline, folds, dimensions. Then a cutaway shows the weave, the weft thread turns into the order's route from Lagos to the customer, and a QC check and red stamp close the title block. At the end the label leaves the drawing as a real object.
2. **The title block as story.** Locked on the title block for most of the film. Its rows fill one by one (ENQUIRY, QUOTE, PAID, WOVEN, QC, SHIPPED) like revision rows, and the stamp gives the verdict. This is strong on process, but it never shows the product, which is the label.
3. **A teardown in reverse.** It opens on a finished garment collar with its label sewn in. Parts are erased until only the label's centre line is left. This is elegant, but it ends on an absence, which is wrong for a promo.

**Why 1:** the product is the hero and gets drawn with care. The business's real selling points (precision, weaving in Lagos, WhatsApp updates, hand QC) each become one figure on one sheet. The red stamp gives a natural verdict.

## 2. Logline and arc

*A customer's message becomes a drawing, the drawing becomes thread, and the thread becomes a woven label that is checked, stamped and sent out into the world.*

- **Setup (0–12.5 s):** an empty sheet. One centre line crosses it, and the label is drafted from a WhatsApp-style message bubble.
- **Development (12.5–27.5 s):** Detail B cuts into the weave. Warp threads shed on the beat and the weft passes through, step by step like an escapement. The weft runs out of the detail and becomes the order route, LAGOS → UK, with a balloon per status.
- **Turn (27.5–35 s):** the QC row ticks off by hand, a signature goes on, then **silence**, then the red **APPROVED** stamp.
- **Ending (35–48 s):** pull back to the whole sheet. The label turns in oblique projection, then becomes the one real thing: a soft white woven label lifting off the paper with a shadow. The lights go off, and the lines glow faintly.

## 3. Benchmark

- **Learn from:** 19th-century patent drawings (figure numbering, part balloons, fine-line hatching); the drafting-order reveals of end-credit technical-drawing title sequences (layers per beat, glowing nib).
- **Do not take:** any characters, shots, logos or melodies from those works, and nothing from the Blueprint demo's story.

## 6. Cue map (score)

96 bpm, D major / B minor, contrapuntal. Instruments: harpsichord, pizzicato/spiccato strings, bassoon, celesta.

- **Bars 0–2:** solo harpsichord, a one-bar motive (the "centre line").
- **Bars 2–5:** a second voice in imitation (bassoon) and light pizzicato.
- **Bars 5–8:** a fugato, with strings spiccato on 8ths stepping with the weft.
- **Bars 8–11:** full texture and a celesta countermelody on the balloons.
- **Bars 11–13:** instruments drop out, with a written-out ritardando into **silence** at 32.5 s.
- **33.125:** a cadence chord on the stamp.
- **Bars 14–19:** the theme in augmentation, celesta and harpsichord, ending on a held chord through the lights-off.

## 8. Subtitles and titles

- Subtitles are drawing NOTES: a translucent box with double white rules, a "NOTE / n" cell and hand lettering (Architects Daughter), lettered on in about 0.35 s. Each is placed per shot in empty paper, never at the title block. Every note is also lettered into the sheet's GENERAL NOTES column.
- The title is the sheet header: **FEYSE CLOTHING LABELS — DWG. 001 · WOVEN BRAND LABEL · SCALE 4:1**.
- Title-block fields use B612. The signature uses Allura.
- The customer's brand on the label is a placeholder, "YOUR BRAND", so any viewer can picture their own name there.
